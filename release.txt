### Feature: D-term (derivative) for overshoot prevention

When a sudden load change occurs, the inverter ramps up to meet the setpoint but often overshoots past zero into grid export before settling. The existing burst correction catches the initial spike, but the overshoot happens after — when gt is already close to zero but still moving fast.

Added a derivative (D) term that applies braking when:
- `gt` is close to zero (within `D_BRAKE_ZONE` = 100W)
- `gt` is changing fast (derivative > `D_THRESHOLD` = 50W/cycle)

The brake is proportional to the speed of approach: `brake = -d_gt * D_GAIN` (0.3).

Config:
- `D_BRAKE_ZONE = 100` — how close to zero to start braking (Watts)
- `D_THRESHOLD = 50` — minimum derivative to trigger (Watts/cycle)
- `D_GAIN = 0.3` — fraction of derivative to apply as brake

Console flag: `[D:+33]` = D-term applied +33W brake

### Feature: Burst Correction for Sudden Load Spikes

When a load turns on suddenly (e.g. pump consuming ~1kW), the EMA filter lags behind the real grid state, causing strategies to under-correct. Result: inverter exports -100 to -400W to grid for several cycles before catching up.

**Burst detection** in `SetpointCalculator` now fires when the instantaneous grid reading (`gt`) diverges from the filtered value (`filtered_gt`) by more than `BURST_THRESHOLD` (150W). An immediate proportional correction (`BURST_GAIN` = 80% of the spike) is applied, bypassing EMA lag.

Config:
- `BURST_THRESHOLD = 150` — minimum spike to trigger (Watts)
- `BURST_GAIN = 0.8` — fraction of spike to correct immediately

Console flag: `[B:+320]` = burst corrected by +320W
